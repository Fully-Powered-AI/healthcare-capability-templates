# Referring practice meeting brief

Before meeting a referring office that has raised handoff problems, the practice liaison gets one brief: what was promised to that office and whether each promise was kept, the questions its staff keep asking, counted by topic, and a proposed agenda. Counts only, no patient detail; the liaison decides.

*Workflow template · Healthcare · Customer success and account management · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/referring-practice-meeting-brief/)*

## The outcome

The liaison walks in knowing the practice's side of the story before the office tells it. Every promise made to that office is on the page with its status taken from your own log: kept, kept late, overdue, or marked done with nothing to show for it. A promise recorded as kept is flagged when the same subject keeps coming back, so nobody defends a fix that has stopped working.

The office's complaints become numbers. Instead of "they say they call all the time", the brief shows how many questions about missing referrals, repeat document requests and late letters came in the last three months against the three before, and how many are still unanswered. The proposed agenda puts broken promises first and ends each subject with the one question the service owner must answer before anything new is offered in the room.

The brief is about the administrative handoff. It carries no patient detail, quotes no question, describes services only in your approved wording and says nothing about care. Anything about a single patient, a clinical question or how soon patients can be seen goes to the right person, off the agenda.

## Who starts the work, and when

The practice liaison starts it two to five working days before the meeting, once the office has asked to talk; in a practice without a liaison, the practice manager does. The liaison chooses the agenda, takes each confirmation question to its service owner before the meeting, and decides what to say.

## What they supply

- **The meeting:** the office, the date, the length, who attends on each side.
- **What the office raised:** the liaison's note of the call, or the office's message.
- **The commitments log** for that office: what was promised, by whom, due when, done when, and the evidence.
- **The questions log** for that office: each administrative question its staff asked, with a topic and whether it was answered, de-identified.
- **Referral figures,** if you keep them: referrals received each month, how many were acknowledged on time and how many were returned for missing documents.
- **Your approved service descriptions,** and who owns each area of the referral handoff.

## What comes out

One brief in a fixed order: what the office raised; every commitment with its status; the recurring questions by topic, this period against the last; the referral figures; what is held for other teams; a timed agenda for the liaison to choose from; and a short list of things to check. Every figure traces to a file you supplied, and the totals are checked before the brief is returned.

The brief promises nothing, sends nothing and changes no log. It ends by saying so.

## What has to change for your company

Name the owner of each part of the handoff: referral intake, scheduling, visit letters, the contact route, patient-specific requests, clinical questions and anything about referral arrangements. Match the column names to your own commitments log, questions log and referral figures. Set how many questions make a topic recurring, your target for answering a referring office, and how long your liaison meetings run.

Decide what counts as evidence that a promise was kept; the template treats "done" with nothing recorded as unconfirmed. Keep the approved descriptions current, because the brief will not describe a service any other way. Use it only in an AI product your practice has approved for this information.
